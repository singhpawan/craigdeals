This is a critical moment for you and your team. The CTO's primary concerns will be:
1.  **Risk & Stability:** What is on fire? What will break? How do we prevent any disruption, especially with a live TSA?
2.  **Assessment:** Who on this team is competent? Who can I trust? Who are the leaders?
3.  **Strategic Alignment:** Is this expensive "new platform" a vanity project, or is it critical? Does it align with *my* (the CTO's) strategy?

You are not just giving a status update; you are auditioning. You need to project stability, confidence, and strategic ownership.

Here is a plan to prepare for that call.

### Your Mindset: The "Stable Partner"

Do not go into this call as a subordinate waiting for instructions. Go in as the competent leader who has the situation under control. You are not the person adding to the CTO's problems; you are the person *solving* them. You are the source of stability and forward momentum.

---

### Your Preparation: A 4-Part Structure

Organize your thoughts and materials into a "briefing document" for yourself. Even if you don't read from it, the act of writing it will solidify your thoughts. Structure it around these four key areas.

#### Part 1: The Team & Mission (The 1-Minute Opener)

Start with confidence. Briefly define who your team is and what its mission is, framed in business terms.

* **Acknowledge the situation (professionally):** "First, I want to acknowledge the leadership change. My team and I are professionals, we are 100% focused on our objectives, and we are committed to ensuring a stable and successful transition."
* **State your commitment:** "My goal today is to give you a transparent, "no-fluff" overview of our entire portfolio, our immediate risks, and the opportunities ahead. I am prepared to lead this team forward and partner with you to execute."

#### Part 2: The 3-Pillar Status (Risk, Build, Maintain)

This is the core of the meeting. Organize your entire update around the three initiatives you mentioned. Address them in order of the CTO's most likely priority: Risk first.

**Pillar 1: The Fire — Worldpay TSA (Operational Risk)**

The CTO will care most about this. A TSA failure means legal penalties, financial impact, and operational chaos.

* **Status:** "The TSA is our highest operational priority." Give a RAG (Red/Amber/Green) status. Be honest.
* **Deliverables:** "We have $X$ critical milestones remaining before the $Y$ exit date. Our next major deadline is $Z$, which involves [describe the data migration/separation]."
* **Risks:** Clearly identify the top 1-2 risks. (e.g., "Our biggest risk is data validation for the separated financial records. We are mitigating this by...")
* **Key Dependencies:** "To meet our deadline, we have a critical dependency on [Other Team/Vendor]. My former boss was the executive sponsor for that relationship. I need your support to ensure that escalation path remains clear."

**Pillar 2: The Business — Legacy Products (Revenue & Stability)**

This is the "keep the lights on" work that funds everything else. Show you are a safe pair of hands.

* **Scope:** "My team maintains the data infrastructure for legacy products A, B, and C, which collectively support $X\%$ of current revenue."
* **Health:** "Our operations are stable. Our key metrics are $X$ uptime and $Y$ data accuracy SLA, both of which are currently green."
* **Risk/Tech Debt:** Briefly mention the biggest risk. (e.g., "The primary risk here is our aging reporting module. It's stable, but it's the main driver for our *new* data platform initiative, as it is costly to maintain and slow to adapt.") This perfectly transitions you to the next topic.

**Pillar 3: The Future — New Data Platform (Strategic Value)**

This is the project most at risk of being cut or changed by a new leader. You must immediately tie it to business value and align it with the CTO's likely goals (cost-saving, efficiency, scalability).

* **The "Why" (Business Case):** Do NOT start with the tech. Start with the problem. "The new data platform solves two critical business problems: 1) It reduces the high maintenance cost and tech debt of our legacy systems, and 2) It unlocks [AI/Analytics/Self-Service] capabilities for the business, which our current stack cannot support."
* **Status:** "We are currently in [Phase 2 of 4]. The core infrastructure is built, and we are beginning data ingestion pipeline development."
* **The Pivot/Ask:** This is your chance to show strategic alignment. "This platform was the key strategic initiative under our former CDAO. I see this as a perfect moment to review the roadmap to ensure it 100% aligns with *your* broader technology strategy. I have a 1-page summary of the architecture and business case, and I’d like to schedule time for a deeper dive with you or your enterprise architect."

#### Part 3: Team Assessment (Show You're a Leader)

The CTO is inheriting an organization and needs to know who the key players are and what the risks are.

* **Morale:** Be direct but positive. "The team is processing the news, but they are resilient and focused on the work. My leadership team and I are in constant communication to ensure everyone feels supported and understands the mission hasn't changed."
* **Key Talent / Flight Risks:** "I want to highlight two individuals who are *critical* to the Worldpay TSA: [Name, Role] and [Name, Role]. They are dedicated professionals, and my top priority is ensuring they remain engaged and supported." This shows you know who matters and are proactively managing your talent.
* **Immediate Gaps:** "With [Former Boss] gone, the primary gaps are [e.g., executive sponsorship for the TSA] and [e.g., final budget approval for the new platform vendors]. I am prepared to step in and manage these, but I will need your delegation and sign-off."

#### Part 4: The Close (Your "Audition")

End the conversation by reiterating your commitment and capability. This is not the time to be modest.

* **The "Ask":** "What are your primary concerns for this organization that I can help you solve immediately?"
* **The "Offer":** "I know this team, I know the technology, and I know the business priorities. I am fully prepared to lead this function, manage these critical projects to completion, and provide the stability our team needs. I am ready to step up and be your key partner in making this data organization a success."

### Quick "Dos" and "Don'ts"

* **DO:** Talk in terms of **Risk, Cost, and Revenue**.
* **DON'T:** Gossip or speak negatively about your former boss. It's unprofessional and makes you look untrustworthy.
* **DO:** Have your facts straight. Know your deadlines, metrics, and budget numbers.
* **DON'T:** Bring problems without proposed solutions or mitigations.
* **DO:** Project calm, confident leadership. The CTO is looking for someone to reduce their stress, not add to it.
* **DON'T:** Get lost in technical jargon. Speak the language of the business and the CTO (architecture, scalability, alignment, execution).
