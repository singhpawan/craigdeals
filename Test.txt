Subject: Risk Mitigation & Strategic Alignment: EDAI Snowflake Platform Ownership

Leadership Team,

I am fully supportive of the EDAI mission and committed to our teams' collective success. As we scale the EDAI (Azure/Databricks) platform, we have reached a critical juncture regarding Snowflake environment ownership. To ensure long-term stability, I want to highlight several significant concerns regarding technical debt, security, and operational continuity.

1. Strategic Risks: Fragmentation and Technical Debt
Architectural Drift: Managing two Snowflake environments under different teams will inevitably lead to fragmented standards, making long-term maintenance significantly more expensive.

Inefficient Engineering: We are observing a trend of "pushing problems downstream." Critical data issues (e.g., missing primary keys) are being addressed via inefficient Snowflake workarounds rather than being resolved at the Databricks source.

Performance Bottlenecks: Specific architectural decisions—such as the use of SCD Global Lookup Tables and Dynamic Tables—have proven non-performant in our benchmark testing, even at low scale. Proceeding with this design will lead to immediate scaling bottlenecks and unpredictable consumption costs.

2. Operational Dependencies & Expertise Gap
Alignment Gap: There is a discrepancy between the perceived independence of the AA platform team and the daily reality of their dependency on the CDP Snowflake engineering team.

"Shadow Engineering": Despite the "no dependency" mandate, my team continues to provide daily, unrecorded support to the EDAI project.

Resource Constraints & Retention: My team is currently operating at limited bandwidth due to three recent departures. Providing "shadow engineering" that is not accounted for in our PI planning jeopardizes our committed deliverables and negatively impacts the retention of our remaining talent.

Execution Lag: Over the last two months, progress has been marginal, with several regressive changes made to the environment.

3. Security & Governance Concerns
Risk Profile: Granting privileged access (AccountAdmin) to external contractors without a history of Snowflake administration creates a significant audit risk and violates the principle of least privilege.

Consistency: For context, we have historically denied this level of access even to experienced LEIT contractors who have been with the organization for a significant time.

4. Future Integration Costs
Our ultimate goal is for the CDP and EDAI Snowflake environments to interoperate seamlessly. While independent execution may seem faster today, it creates "technical silos." Every week the EDAI platform operates under divergent standards adds weeks of future work required to make them compatible—work we are currently trying to minimize.

Recommendation
To ensure the success of EDAI, we must formalize our "Ways of Working."

If the AA team owns the platform, they must be fully self-sufficient and assume all operational risks.

If my team is expected to ensure the platform’s success, we must have authority over its architecture and standards.

With the proper guardrails and authority in place, we can collaborate effectively to ensure a high-performing, integrated data ecosystem.
